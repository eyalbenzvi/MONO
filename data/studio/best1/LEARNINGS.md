# best1: what worked, what didn't, and how to beat 8.2

The run set out to make the shop's best-scored design (record: Pluto Was Framed, 8.2). Four candidates went
through blind panels of six judges (up to three rounds, fixes only where two or more agreed). None beat 8.2.

## Findings

1. **The record was set by a different panel, so the two scales don't match.** Pluto (8.2) was scored by five judges: no
   head designer at RVCA, and a customer persona matched to the design ("30-year-old into space, old punk flyers and
   band tees"), over up to five rounds. best1 used six judges, including RVCA, with a generic everyday customer. RVCA
   is the hardest judge (6.73 on average across best1; the everyday customer gave 7.73). Without RVCA, How to Go to Bed's round 2
   scores 8.2, level with the record.
2. **Panel noise is about ±0.25.** How to Go to Bed went from 8.08 to 7.83 between rounds with only small changes.
   A record needs a clear margin or the mean of two panels.
3. **Fixing can make things worse.** Three of the four designs peaked before their last round: the dog (r2), Probably Fine (r2,
   then cutting icons cost 0.5) and The Box (r1, then three rounds of decline). The loop shipped the last version, not
   the best one.
4. **Under this same six-judge panel, dense engraved plates scored highest in round 1:** Octopus (ready3, 8.08:
   a natural-history engraving) and Joiner's Tools (ready2, 8.00: a numbered catalogue page), both Gemini. In
   best1 the judges kept calling the work "souvenir clip art", "a narrow column in empty space", "even line
   weight", "timid". Wit carried the scores; craft held them back.
5. **The joke has to land at three metres without explanation.** Each time a judge had to work the joke out, the score dropped. Pisa's
   tilted stamp read as a misprint, 3.97° read as a price, and The Box's three panels felt small. The dog's
   "Turn around." ×4 landed at once, and was the best-liked.
6. **A dense black-tee design does well.** Pluto (black, halftone collage) and Wok Hei (7.92, black, halftone) are both at the top.

## Principles, not templates (the owner's note)

The lessons are reasons, not formats to copy. A past winner's format (a field guide, a stamp, numbered steps)
reused is a remix, and remixes and museum copies have already cost the shop. What the scores reward:
one idea felt in a second; the picture carries the point; a second look pays off; presence; freshness; truth;
few strong elements that can be made well; something the wearer is glad to say; the single ink used as a medium.
The next competition's brief (best2) states these as principles, bans the spent formats, and asks the rankers
to score the printed shirt, not the sentence.

## How to beat 8.2 (first draft, superseded by the principles above)

- **Format:** a dense, engraved plate (a field guide, specimen sheet or catalogue page) whose subject is the
  joke, so the craft of 4 and the instant wit of 5 come together. Pasta: A Field Guide (8.0) is the closest so far.
- **Source:** Gemini engravings (the run's 8+ designs), not flat ChatGPT line.
- **Process:**
  - Keep the best-scoring version: if a round scores lower, revert it.
  - Confirm a record with a second, independent panel; the record is the mean of the two.
  - Decide which panel is the standard. Either re-score Pluto under the six-judge panel, or run the five-judge panel the record used.
- **Ideas in this format:**
  - Sleeping dogs as a field guide (The Donut, The Superman, The Croissant…), building on the run's best idea.
  - Felis in a box: cats in boxes as specimen plates.
  - Leaning towers of the world, each with its lean angle.

## best2: Murmuration, cancelled

The second competition's winner (7.83 from the rankers): a murmuration of starlings forming one giant starling.
- Panel scores: round 1 6.0, round 2 6.75. Two judges passed round 2.
- The giant bird read only when its body was a solid dark shape. The judges called that "a cut-out, not a flock".
- Built from small birds alone, as the idea requires, it did not read at all, at any density tried. With one ink,
  small figures with sky between them make an even texture that blends into the sky behind, and with no tonal step the large
  shape never separates from it.
- The owner's rule was to make it as intended or drop it, so it was dropped.

Lesson: an idea that rests on a visual effect (a figure made of figures, an optical trick) must be prototyped in
one ink before it is chosen. The rankers scored the sentence; the effect could not be printed.

## best3–best6: "No.", into the shop

The third competition (100 ideas, six rankers) put "No." first: a cat lying on the word, pushing the full stop off
the end of the line. Built five ways, each through the six-judge panel:

| Version | Best average |
|---|---|
| Gemini cat, lettering and grain drawn in code (best3) | 7.08 |
| Gemini redraw, cat and letters in one hand, thin cuts (best4, round 3) | 7.42 |
| Gemini redraw, letters nearly solid (best5) | 7.17 |
| Gemini redraw, paw on the full stop, deadpan stare (best6, round 1) | 7.25 (shipped, the owner's pick) |

- Code-drawn lettering next to a drawn cat never got past "two hands": every panel asked for the letters in the
  cat's hand. Having the image model draw the whole design at once worked better than any fix made here.
- The full stop decided the joke: until it sat solid on the baseline just past the O, the judges read "NO" plus a
  ball. Pinholes to keep it under the no-slab rule read grey; the owner allowed the full stop and the letters solid.
- The idea plateaued between 7 and 7.5. Beating 8.2 needs a stronger idea, not more polish on this one.
