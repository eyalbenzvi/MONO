# MONO · black and white tees, one ink

Swipe ten tees and the shop edits itself to your taste. Every print is a single ink on a black or white tee. A vector recommendation engine learns your taste as you swipe, then opens the shop as **your edit**.

## Catalogue: 1,377 tees in 10 categories

The catalogue is offline and deterministic. `scripts/generateCatalog.ts` (seeded; two runs are byte-identical) builds every design it has ever made — so ids never move — then takes out what the reviews retired (with the reason, logged per run) and writes:

- `public/prints/print_<n>.svg`: the drawn prints, 3:4 single-ink SVG (minified; `scripts/gen/minify.ts`); `print_<n>.webp`: the photographs and archive works, each a true one-ink halftone (see "Photographs")
- `data/shirts.index.json`: the lean index the app bundles (`lib/catalog.ts`): id-derivable fields left out, stored by column, feature values one symbol each (~90 KB). Its head carries the feature keys (checked against `FEATURE_KEYS` on load — a stale index stops the app instead of decoding nonsense), the taste-test ids, the drop epoch, the shard size and every shard's content hash.
- `public/data/details-<k>.<hash>.json`: descriptions, subjects, real print sizes and precomputed "similar prints" (`shardSize` designs per shard), fetched on demand (`lib/details.ts`); the hash in the name makes each file cacheable forever.
- `data/shirts.json`: the full catalogue, read at build time only (product page metadata and props via `lib/catalogServer.ts`, link-preview images)

```bash
npm run generate       # rebuilds all of it (and the published index), byte-identical on every run
npm run audit:prints   # renders every print in Chromium and flags text that overflows, overlaps,
                       # crosses a rule or is squeezed; then refuses any print that lands as a solid block
```

**The content overhaul (the brand book).** No print may land on the tee as a solid block of ink (`scripts/gen/quality.ts` `solidBlock`: a filled slab, a panel with shapes cut out of it, a frame of ink; refused at generation and by `audit:prints`). A new design also may not carry a continuous dense area over 15% of the print (rule f, `MASS_AREA`: a pale sky printed as an oval of mesh on a black tee); it is measured on every print and holds back the studio's prints, the content waves' candidates and an upload offered to the catalogue, not the catalogue already on sale. A scene drawn to its edges gets an ending (`scripts/studio/oneink.py --edge horizon|rect|circle|arch|oval`; Design Guidelines, section 04). Parody homages, souvenir landmarks, clip art, pixel sunsets, scribbles, random words, dated stamps, 5- and 6-fold star rosettes, slivers and paper edges are gone; near-duplicates are cut to two per family and one per subject; photographs with people, brands, lettering or insignia were taken out or retitled generically (`data/curation/retired.json`, `titles.json`). Every raster print is a one-ink halftone. Titles name what the print shows. The shop's first 24 are a fixed, hand-pinned window (`data/curation/window.json`).

Generator modules: `scripts/gen/core.ts` (randomness, geometry, contracts) · `legacy.ts`, `expansion.ts`, `set3/` (the first sets, ids 1–2800, mostly retired) · `set5/` (ids 3401–3830: star charts, curves, plants by rule, ornament) · `set7/` (ids from 7001: designs made from real data) · `quality.ts` (the solid-block check and the quality score) · `retire.ts` (the reviews' removals) · `titles.ts` (titles that name what you see) · `categories.ts` (the shop's categories) · `describe.ts` (closing lines, each used at most 20 times).

### Photographs

The photographs (ids 2801–3400; patent models from the archive join them) are real photographs from **Smithsonian Open Access**, all released as **CC0**. Each design keeps where it came from: the photographer or museum credit and a link to its collection record, shown under the description on the product page ("Photo: … · Smithsonian Open Access, CC0 · Source record") and in `data/shirts.json` (`photo`).

- `scripts/photos/fetchPhotos.ts` (needs the network, run by hand; downloads cached in `node_modules/.cache/mono-photos`): `candidates` reads the museum metadata from the public bucket (`s3://smithsonian-open-access`) and keeps CC0 images of animals (National Zoo) and of aircraft, engines and instruments (Air and Space); `prep` makes each print from the full-resolution original — **the whole photograph, never cropped**, greyscale, levels stretched, fitted into the 28 × 37 cm print area (a wide aircraft stays whole and simply prints as a wide picture); an object shot against a plain studio backdrop is **cut out** (`scripts/photos/cutout.py`, the rembg `isnet-general-use` model) so it sits on the tee like the drawn prints; `sheet` writes numbered contact sheets; `select` takes the best 200 per category and writes `data/photos/photos.json` (credit, record, measures) and the originals `assets/masters/print_<n>.webp` (kept in git: the tone every halftone is made from). Then `scripts/photos/halftone.py` screens each original — photographs, patent models and archive scans — into a true one-ink halftone in `public/prints/print_<n>.webp` (AM round dot, 30 lpi at 45°, 1500 × 2000, alpha only 0 or 255), never touching the original: a photograph's dots are baked in the ink of its tee; strokes print solid, and only a dark mass wider than ~3.5 mm is capped at 80% dot coverage (no solid slab); `data/curation/halftone.json` records each print's mode, tee, the original's and the output's hashes and the recipe.
- `scripts/photos/curation.ts` is the hand review of those contact sheets: what was left out (people in the picture — a keeper's hand holding a frog counts —, busy museum halls, near-duplicates, text-heavy plates) and subject clean-up (the species rather than the zoo name of the animal, record typos, no personal names in titles).
- **Invert.** A drawn print is strictly two-tone, so the other tee colour swaps its two inks. A photograph inverted is a *negative* — so photographs are **never inverted**. Their halftone is baked in the ink of their one tee: white dots lay down the picture's lights on black, black dots its darks on white (`needsInvert()` in `lib/catalog.ts`, applied when the pictures are baked). The original colour is the tee that shows more of the picture (a light picture on black, a dark one on white).

**Pictures are baked at build time** (`scripts/images/bake.ts` → `public/img`, git-ignored; `npm run images`, run by `prebuild` and by `npm run og`; only designs whose print, photo or recipe changed are baked again). For every design in every colour it's sold in: the design on its model photo (`img/m/<n>-<colour>-<w>.webp` at 360/720/1080 px — the print shrunk once with a Lanczos filter, so a halftone averages into tone instead of aliasing into blocks, and laid on the fabric as it prints: black ink multiplied onto white, white ink screened onto black); the flat print (`img/p/…` at 480 px and the exact 1500 px halftone, or the vector file in that colourway); and a close-up of the print's area for zooming (`img/d/…`, 900 px). The page shows each as one plain `<img srcset sizes>` (`lib/images.ts`, `TeeMockup`, `PrintImage`): the browser picks the file, a pinch-zoomed page asks for a bigger one (`usePageZoom`), and a zoomed card lays the close-up over the print's area. Nothing is blended, inverted or resized on the page. The export ships only these (`postbuild` drops `prints/` and `models/`, their inputs). The share image and the link previews are drawn from the same pictures.
- Names are the subject ("Douglas DC-3", "Red Panda"); a second photograph of the same subject is its "Take 2" and joins the first as one design family (variations). All 600 dropped together, the week they were added.

**What each print shows.** `scripts/gen/subject.ts` reads a short subject ("Solar Eclipse", "Potted Cactus", "Great Wave Homage") from each design's own description. Page titles use it — `Northern Pines — Solar Eclipse Line-Art Tee | MONO` — and meta descriptions are the name, the subject, the design's own sentence, the tee and the price (unique for every design; tested). Objects, caricatures and iconic images take the subject as their name's noun ("Everyday Potted Cactus", "Local CEO") instead of filler nouns like "Relic" or "Thing".

**Print quality** (`scripts/gen/quality.ts`): every print is rendered small with resvg and scored 0–100 from its ink coverage, how much of the print it spans and how much detail it has. Prints under 35 (a lone small shape) are *weak*: never in the taste test, the default link preview or the top 96 of any shop order, nor in "picked for you" rows. The ink's bounding box gives each print's real size on the tee (Details on the product page, and the zoom's scale bar).

**Drops.** Every design carries an explicit drop date (weekly Mondays, 40 a drop). "New this week" is judged on the viewer's clock for seven days after it, and `.github/workflows/weekly-rebuild.yml` re-runs the Pages deploy every Monday.

Pictures are drawn as illustrations with halftone and hatch patterns, never grey fills, so every print stays strictly two-colour. That is what makes the black/white colourway swap an exact inversion.

Feature vectors have 17 dimensions. The second set added **pictorial, wit, retro and nature**; the third added **figurative and classic**; the photographs added **photographic** (0.95 on every photograph, 0 on every drawing — a photo lover and a halftone lover are different people). A photograph's other features come from the picture and its category: density and contrast from the picture itself (how much of the print it fills and its tonal range), nature for animals, industrial for engines, retro for the aircraft collection, minimal for an object on a bare tee. The taste test always includes at least one photograph (`getCalibrationQueue(…, mustInclude)`), so the new dimension is measured, not guessed. Stored profiles from before get "photographic" at neutral 0.5 (taste store v4 migration); a profile whose taste test was already finished stays finished, and the photographs in the new taste test are simply dealt next. Old `?taste=` links (32 characters) still open, the photo lean at neutral. Older designs get deterministic values for new dimensions, and stored user profiles are extended with neutral 0.5 rather than reset. The taste test picks one design from each of the ten categories, only prints in the top quarter for quality, a graphic first; taste store v5 keeps anyone who had already answered a whole test finished when its prints changed.

**Design families (variations).** Many generated prints are near-identical: same algorithm, close parameters. The generator gives every design a *visual signature* built from the parameters the eye actually notices: algorithm, word, shape and polarity as categories, plus normalised continuous values such as vanishing point, grid size or fill. It then groups designs with deterministic leader clustering (`FAMILY_THRESHOLD`) into families (the content overhaul left at most two designs in any). For caption designs the text itself is part of the signature. Each shirt carries `family` and `variant` in `data/shirts.json`. At runtime (`lib/catalog.ts`, `lib/deck.ts`):
- **Discover never shows two designs of one family in a run.** Seeing one (swipe, save or dealt card) excludes its siblings. It also avoids repeating an algorithm within 5 cards. The taste test uses 10 different families, and its progress counts families.
- **Shop:** one card per family (the best-ranked one) with a "+N variations" badge. In "For you" order, neighbours never share an algorithm. This is display-only pacing; scores are unchanged.
- **Product page:** a *Variations* strip with the whole family (switching keeps your tee colour, and "back" still returns to the shop). *Similar prints* shows related but different designs, one per algorithm.

**Every design comes in both colourways.** `baseColor` is the original the print was drawn for; the buyer can pick the other tee colour on the product page, the Discover card's details face or the Saved drawer (the bag names the colour; it is changed on the product page). Because prints are strictly two-colour, the reverse colourway is an exact CSS inversion of the same SVG (no extra files); the SKU's colour letter flips (`MN-GEO-B-0001` ↔ `MN-GEO-W-0001`). Bag lines are keyed by design + size + colour. In the shop, the colour switch shows the whole catalog in black or white tees.

Feature vectors are computed from each design's real parameters (line/cell counts, fill ratio, stroke weight, dot coverage, framing) rather than assigned. Tees are exactly 70% black / 30% white. Every print is single-ink: white ink on black tees, black ink on white. The mockup blends the print's ground into the fabric (`screen` / `multiply`) so only the ink shows.

## Flow

The screen works with one thumb: the only thing at the top is the wordmark (it leads home), and navigation is a quiet text tab bar at the bottom, **Discover · Shop · Make · Bag · You** (`components/TabBar.tsx`; the active tab is white and underlined; on its own page a tap scrolls back to the top, and under it, on a tee or an editor, a tap goes back to it). The bar is always there but for the first taste test; a page's own buy bar sits just above it (`--tabbar`). Every overlay is one bottom sheet (`components/Sheet.tsx`: a portal on the body, a grabber to drag it away, the X, the primary action at the bottom). Each is a step in the history (`hooks/useHistorySheet.ts`: `#buy`, `#details-card`, `#taste`, `#share`, `#zoom`, `#filter`, `#search`, `#reveal`), so a phone's Back closes it. The toast and the add confirmation sit at the bottom, just above whatever bar is there (`--dock`, `lib/dock.ts`).

1. **Discover and the taste test** (`/`): the first ten cards are the most mutually different prints. One line above the card is never empty and replaces its own words:
   - on card 1: "Ten tees. Keep or pass. We’ll edit the shop to your taste.";
   - on cards 2 to 9: "Learning your taste · N/10";
   - on card 10: "Last one.";
   - once, on card 6, a stylist note ("Noted: dense prints") when the answers lean clearly;
   - for 4 s after each swipe, "Saved · Undo" or "Passed · Undo".

   A 2px line under it shows how far along you are. With too few of either answer the line says what's missing ("Almost there. Keep 2 you’d wear."). During the test there are only Pass and Save; the Buy button between them comes back once the taste is known. The card tints towards Save (light) or Pass (shade) with no words, and leans at most 6°. Finishing opens **the reveal**:
   - the eyebrow "Your taste", the archetype ("The Maximalist") and one sentence in words (`tasteSentence` in `lib/taste.ts`);
   - three tees and "Your shop is now edited around this.";
   - "See your edit" and "Keep swiping" (Back does the same).

   After the test, the line reads "Your taste · The … ›" and opens Your taste (the same summary as on You).

   The Buy sheet (`components/BuySheet.tsx`) sells the tee on the card without leaving the deck: Black · White · Both · $90, the sizes, "Buy now · M · $50" straight to checkout, "Add to bag", and a "View tee" row.

   A card's reason, once the taste is known: "Because you saved X" (centered cosine ≥ 0.7), else the traits in common in words ("Dense prints · nature"); the explore cards say "Something different".
2. **Shop** (`/shop`): a quiet heading, "Our pick" before the taste test and "Your edit" after it, and the grid. The first card of your edit is the reveal's first pick and says "Top pick". The controls are one row at the bottom, above the tab bar: "Black · White" (the tee colour, a toggle), "Search" and "Filter" (the categories in a sheet, each with its count). Cards show the picture, square and full-bleed, and the name on up to two lines: no price, no badge, no quick add. The heart shows with a mouse, or once a tee is saved. The counts are one unit, tees with their variations, so "All 1,377" and "Show 1,377 tees" agree. Before the test the order is "Our pick" (the generator's fixed editorial rank; its first 24 are the shop window, the same for everyone). The top of the grid is diversified (`diversify` in `lib/catalog.ts`): never three of one category or tee colour in a row, no repeated algorithm, and a wildcard every 8th card (a taste probe, not labelled).
3. **Search** (a sheet; `/` opens it on a desktop): the field sits at the foot of the sheet, next to the keyboard, with suggestions above it and, while it's empty, the prepared parameters (Look, Subject, Made, Era, Source; Your edit once the taste is known; your last five searches). It searches each design's title, subject, description, artist, museum, scientific name and style, forgives typos ("Showing lighthouse · Search for “lihgthouse”") and never ends empty ("No exact match for “x”. Closest:"). Enter, or a filter chosen, shows the grid; the control row then names the search, with × to clear it. It runs offline on a static index built at build time (`scripts/tools/searchIndex.ts` → `public/data/search.<hash>.json`, ~77 KB gzipped), loaded only when the shop is idle or search opens; nothing typed is sent anywhere. The address carries it: `/shop/?q=…&f=kind:value&like=<id>`.
4. **Product** (`/shop/[id]`): from top to bottom on a phone:
   - the picture, full-bleed and square-cornered (4:5, never taller than leaves the title, colour and sizes above the fold), swiped from the tee to the flat print, with thin dots; a tap opens the zoom in the view on screen;
   - the title, with Share beside it;
   - one Why line, "Top pick · retro, geometric" or "For you · …", only when it's true (a tap opens Your taste);
   - "Make your own →" when the design has a made-for-you version;
   - Black · White · **Both · $90** (the pair, `PAIR_PRICE`);
   - "Kids’ sizes" and "Size guide" on one row, then the sizes (44px);
   - one trust line from the policy ("Arrives Wed 7 – Tue 13 Oct · Free returns and exchanges within 30 days · Organic cotton, 220 gsm");
   - Details.

   On a desktop, two columns: the pictures on the left and the information sticky on the right. The sticky bar says "[verb] · [size] · [price]" and always shows the price: "Add to bag · $50" (tapped without a size, the sizes are highlighted, focus moves to the first and "Select your size." is announced), "Add to bag · M · $50", "Add the pair · M · $90", "Complete the pair · +$40". After adding it reads "In your bag · Checkout" until the size or colour changes, and never adds a second one silently. The size is remembered once chosen anywhere. A shared link opens with one line, "Shared with you", and ✕. No "← Shop" row: Back (the browser or the edge swipe) returns to the grid where it was, and the Shop tab, always at the foot, goes to the grid.
5. **Bag & checkout** (`/cart`):
   - **The bag**: "← Shop", the lines (size and Remove as 44px targets), the summary with "Shipping $10 · free with 2 tees" for a single tee, or "Shipping · Free" from `FREE_SHIPPING_TEES` (two tees, a pair counting as two), and the delivery window with the returns that apply. "Checkout · $X" sits pinned at the bottom.
   - **An empty bag**: "Your bag is empty." with "Start the taste test" (before the test) or "See your edit".
   - **Checkout**: one line for the order ("2 tees · $90 · Arrives …"), then the form in the order people fill it (email, name, country, address, city, postcode, with the right autocomplete, capitalisation and keyboard hints). The last field's Go submits.
     - **The country** is guessed from the time zone, then the browser's language region, else "Select a country". A city or postcode that can't go with the chosen country shows "Check your country" (`lib/checkout.ts`).
     - **Errors**: one summary ("2 fields need attention") moves focus to the first problem; each error is a short line under its field.
     - **Placing the order**: "Place order · $X" is pinned at the bottom, with the one line "Preview store. No payment is taken and nothing ships."
   - **The confirmation**: "Thank you, Ada." and "Order M-… is confirmed.", then "Continue shopping". What was typed survives going back to the bag (kept in memory, never saved). An upload awaiting its check adds up to 2 days to the window.
6. **You** (`/me/`): one page in three parts.
   - **Your taste**: the archetype, the sentence, up to five traits in words, "Share your taste" (a 9:16 card and a `/?taste=…` link) and "Reset taste" (the only reset, apart from the end of the deck).
   - **Saved**: in full, with "+" per row (the remembered size, else the tee's page to select one), × with Undo, a swipe right to add or left to remove, and "Share list" (`/shop/?list=…`).
   - **Orders & settings**: the last order, the size, About, and Clear data.

   A friend's taste link starts Discover with "Your friend is The …. Swipe ten to compare." and the reveal says what you share ("You and your friend share: geometric shapes.") or "You and your friend see it differently." There are no levels, streaks, daily goals or percentages anywhere.
7. **Prices and promises**: the four prices live in `lib/prices.ts` (tee $50, the pair $90; made for you $75, its pair $130), and they are shown only where money changes hands: the buy buttons, the Buy sheet and the bag (plus "Both · $90" on the colour picker). Every policy line renders from `lib/store-policy.ts`, and the delivery window from `lib/delivery.ts`, so the product page, Make, the bag, checkout and the structured data always say the same.
8. **Privacy**: the saved "last order" keeps only its number, items and totals, never name, email or address (cart store v3 removes them from older saves). A Content-Security-Policy meta tag limits scripts, images and requests to the site itself (plus the API when configured).
9. **Backend hooks** (`lib/api.ts`): nothing is sent anywhere unless `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL` is set. Without it, no UI that needs one is shown: no email capture, no referrals, no "most swiped".

Discover keeps training after the taste test (80% best match / 20% explore).

## Deploy (GitHub Pages)

**Product pages and scale.** Every product page is pre-rendered by default (1,377 pages; see the numbers below). To keep the export small as the catalog grows, build with `NEXT_PUBLIC_PRERENDER_LIMIT=<N>`: only the top N designs of the editorial rank get a static page at `/shop/<id>/` and a link-preview image (`npm run og` follows the same rule); every other design opens through the client route `/shop/p/?id=<id>`, and an old link to its `/shop/<id>/` lands on the 404, whose one script sends it there (query and hash kept, `lib/notFound.ts`). All in-app links and share links go through `productHref()` in `lib/catalog.ts`. Beyond that, the next step is a server (or edge function) rendering product pages on demand.

**What a page carries.** The catalog index isn't in the JavaScript: `npm run build` first publishes it as `public/data/index.<hash>.json` (`scripts/tools/publishIndex.ts`); every page preloads it and `lib/catalog` fills in when it arrives, while React keeps the server HTML and hydrates once it has (`<CatalogGate>` in AppShell). First Load JS went from ~238 KB to ~182 KB on Discover (the index is ~54 KB gzipped, cached forever under its hash, and code changes no longer re-download it; there's no build timestamp in the bundle either). Icons come from one sprite (`public/icons.svg`, `<Icon name>` + `<use>`), each tee is one baked picture (see "Pictures are baked at build time"), and structured data is written after the build rather than rendered by React (so it isn't repeated in the page's React payload).

**Security.** Each page's Content-Security-Policy meta tag is written after the build, first in `<head>`, with the sha256 of that page's own inline scripts — no `'unsafe-inline'` for scripts (`lib/csp.ts`, `scripts/tools/postbuild.ts`; `e2e/r2-stage6.desktop.spec.ts` checks the main pages run without a violation).

**CI.** `.github/workflows/test.yml` runs the unit tests, types, lint, the build, the smoke run and the Playwright suite on every push to the deployed branches (the Pages workflow itself is unchanged). Tests take their numbers from `scripts/gen/constants.ts` rather than copying them.

**SEO under `/MONO`.** Every page states its canonical URL and Open Graph URL (`pageMeta` in `lib/seo.ts`); the home page carries Organization and WebSite JSON-LD, product pages a ProductGroup (colour × size variants with offers, shipping and return policy) and a breadcrumb; `og:type=product` and `product:price:*` are written after the build (`scripts/tools/postbuild.cjs`, which also fails the build if a pre-rendered product has no link-preview image). Pre-rendered HTML carries an H1 on the home page and the shop, a real "Show more" link (`/shop/?page=2`) and links to variations and similar prints on every product page. The robots.txt paths include the base path, but crawlers only read robots.txt at a host's root — under `github.io/MONO/` it has no effect until the site has its own domain; the sitemap is linked from every page instead. A CI build without `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_ORIGIN` fails (it would publish localhost URLs); locally it falls back to `http://localhost:3000`.

`.github/workflows/deploy-pages.yml` builds a static export (`out/`) and publishes it on every push. One-time setup: repo **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**. The site is served at `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`.

## Sharing

Every tee can be shared from the product page (Share, beside the title), the Discover card's details, and Saved on You ("Share list"). The **share sheet** (`components/ShareSheet.tsx`) works like this:

- **Share image, drawn in the browser** (`lib/shareImage.ts`): the tee in the chosen colour with its print, name and site (no price: prices are shown only where money changes hands), as a *Story* (1080×1920) or *Post* (1080×1080) PNG. The print SVG is recoloured by swapping its two inks, so it works in every browser. The image is rendered when the sheet opens, so the file is ready inside the tap's user activation.
- **Share…**: the device share sheet (Web Share API) with the image, message ("“X”, black tee. MONO"; made for you: "Made on MONO: “X”, black tee.") and link. On phones this reaches WhatsApp, Instagram (story, feed or DM), TikTok, Facebook, Messages and anything else installed.
- **Instagram / TikTok**: they have no web share intent. Where file sharing is available they open the share sheet with the image. Otherwise the image is saved, the link copied, and short how-to steps are shown.
- **WhatsApp, Facebook, Telegram, X, Email, SMS**: direct share intents (`lib/share.ts`), plus Copy link and Save image.
- **Links** open the product page in the shared colour and are tagged with the channel: `/shop/mono-2002/?c=white&ref=whatsapp`. The recipient sees one line above the title, "Shared with you", with ✕; the page's action stays Add to bag. Saved and then taken to Discover, the taste test starts from it ("Saved X. Ten more and your edit is ready."). The tags are removed from the address bar. A friend's taste link (`/?taste=…`) opens Discover with "Your friend is The …. Swipe ten to compare."; the reveal then names what you share, never a percentage.
- **Link previews**: every product page has Open Graph / Twitter tags pointing at a 1200×630 PNG (`npm run og`, `scripts/generateOgImages.ts`, rendered with resvg and palette-compressed to about 28 KB). The Pages workflow renders them before the build, in batches of short-lived workers (resvg's native memory isn't reclaimed by the JS garbage collector). They aren't committed (`public/og` is git-ignored).

## How the engine works (`lib/recommendation.ts`)

- **Vectors:** each shirt has 10 features in `[0, 1]` (`types/shirt.ts`). The user vector starts at `0.5` on every axis.
- **Learning:** like → `v + 0.15·(f − v)`, dislike → `v − 0.08·(f − v)`, clamped to `[0, 1]`.
- **Cold start:** the first 10 cards come from `getCalibrationQueue`, which uses greedy farthest-point sampling on centered cosine to pick the most mutually orthogonal prints.
- **After calibration:** `getNextCard` rolls 80% greedy (highest match) / 20% explore (the print most orthogonal to the current profile).
- **Match score:** `cosineSimilarity` returns plain cosine as 0–100%. Plain cosine between positive vectors bunches up in the 75–95% band, so the badge uses `matchScore`, which blends in the cosine of the vectors centered on 0.5 as the profile moves away from neutral. Both numbers are shown in the debug panel.
- **Real-time deck:** after every swipe, the card already showing underneath stays put and everything behind it is re-ranked with the new vector.

State lives in three Zustand stores:

- `store/tasteStore.ts` (persisted as `mono-taste`): saved and passed designs, the taste vector, the last 500 swipe events, every design seen (so the deck never repeats a family), the deck, undo info and onboarding flags.
- `store/cartStore.ts` (persisted as `mono-cart`): the bag, the last order, and size / colour picks.
- `store/useUiStore.ts` (memory only): card flip, queued swipes, undo animation, toast, zoom, shop filters, header, debug.

State read back from `localStorage` goes through a guard (`sanitizeTaste` / `sanitizeCart`): malformed or unknown entries fall back to defaults. Sessions from the old single store (`mono-session-v1`, v3–v6) are migrated once into the split stores (`store/legacySession.ts`). Other tabs' writes are picked up through the `storage` event. Product events go to `window.dataLayer` via `lib/analytics.ts` (no third-party script; a tag manager can forward them).

### Upload (`/make/yours/`)

Two screens, one white button each, the picture always in view and never blank (while converting it shows the last print, or the source faded and grey, under a status pill). The step is in the hash (none before a file, `#print`, `#size`; an old `#start` lands on Your print), so a phone's Back walks back through them; the file in hand is kept on the device as a draft, so a reload comes back to it.

1. **Your print, before a file**: the tee (the model photo every upload is shown on) with the Full print area marked in dashes, "Your print here", exactly where the print lands; a tap on it opens the file chooser (so does the white button, "Choose a picture", or a file dropped on the page). Under it the heading "Your print", one line ("Any picture, printed in one ink. JPG, PNG, WebP or SVG."), why a file was refused (any picture the browser opens is taken, GIF, BMP, AVIF or HEIC included where it can; the kind is read from the first bytes, so a missing or wrong extension doesn't matter; the short side needs 600 px, 900 for Full; a brand in the file name only keeps it from becoming the title), and "Carry on with …" for a draft. The converter's regression examples (`scripts/tools/buildYoursExamples.ts`: a CC0 Smithsonian photograph and a drawing made in code, `tests/upload/examples.test.ts`) are no longer shown on the page.
2. **Your print, with a file**: Style (Dots · Lines · Drawing, a photograph only: Drawing reads a sketch shot on paper as line work), Print size (Full · Small) and Tee (Black · White · Both) as pills of text, the stage showing the result; an option that won't print is left out; the tee rule's pick is marked "Suggested". Style and Print size stay when a print fails (Tee waits for a print). "Edit photo" crops (free, square, 3:4, 4:3; a shape too small to print at is greyed out), turns, mirrors, sets light and contrast and has "Stronger", with Save always in view; "Original" shows the source. A first print that fails takes the first fix that passes and says so in one line ("We've set Small."); later, a failing print shows itself, says why and offers only the fixes that pass (Stronger, Use Lines / Use Dots, Use Small / Use Full, Bolder; Crop tighter opens Edit photo). The reasons say what's wrong in plain terms and never suggest a thing the page doesn't offer. Typed words are not a Upload option.
3. **Size**: the title (text with a pencil; a tap edits it), the summary with "Change" (back to Your print), the size, one line saying adding confirms you made it or have permission (with "What we won't print"), "Add to bag · L · $75" (Edit from the bag opens the line as it is, its tee and its size, and "Save changes" puts the new print in each line it was in, keeping sizes and quantities; Both adds the other colour, one tee makes the pair that tee, and a line removed meanwhile is added again: `lib/cart` `editUpload`). An edit keeps no draft. A picture in hand is kept as a draft: left for another page and back in the same tab, Upload opens it where it was; a later visit offers "Carry on with …". A file picked on Your print and refused says why there, the picture in hand kept.

- **Conversion** runs in a same-origin module worker (`lib/upload/worker.ts`; no blob workers, the CSP unchanged): SVGs are sanitised and rasterised, drawings get a Sauvola threshold and clean-up, photographs a port of `scripts/photos/halftone.py` (AM round dot, 30 lpi, 45°, 8–80%) or Canny lines. The result is a 1500 × 2000 one-bit raster, EXIF dropped.
- **The quality bar** (`lib/upload/measure.ts`) is the catalogue's: `solidBlock`, `assessPrint` ≥ 53, coverage 1–45%, detail, the thinnest line and gap (5th percentile of the skeleton's width), and the nearest catalogue design by dHash: within 6 bits the file passes a person first, who can refuse a copy (never refused on the hash alone: sparse or wide prints hash close to designs they're nothing like). A plain frame round a photograph (a scan's border, a letterbox) is trimmed before the dots, so it never prints as a solid ring. Bold strokes and filled shapes in line work and SVGs are meshed (the same 80% screen as a photograph's darkest dots) instead of refused as solid ink. Reasons live in `lib/upload/reasons.ts`.
- **The bag** (cart store v6): an upload line carries `{ id, mode, size, hash }`; its raster stays in IndexedDB (`lib/upload/store.ts`). A line whose raster is gone leaves the bag with one line saying so. The last order keeps only the mode.
- **The review is simulated** (`lib/upload/review.ts`): queued, then cleared after 20 s; a file near a threshold passes a person first. `?review=refuse:<logo|artwork|person|explicit|hate|words|quality>` or `?review=person` (or the debug panel) force a state. The status shows on the confirmation and in `/me`; a refused file can be replaced on the same order line.
- **The Open Call** (simulated): a cleared file of the catalogue tier can be offered; 30 s later it is accepted (quality ≥ 72, nothing within Hamming 12) and joins this device's shop as `mono-u-…` with its credit, ranked by taste, never in Discover. `/me` lists "Your offers" with Withdraw. `STORE_POLICY.openCall`: $6 a tee, $10 a pair.
- **Deletion**: a raster 30 days after its review cleared (unless accepted), an abandoned upload after 7 days; "Clear data" in `/me` clears IndexedDB too. Sharing an upload shares its picture only, with a link to `/make/yours/`.
- **Privacy**: inputs stay on the device. Analytics get the template or kind, never content (`make_open`, `make_filter {groups}`, `customize_apply`, `voice_record {ok}`, `line_draw {points_bucket}`, `upload_start {kind}`, `upload_preview {class, tier}`, `upload_refused {reason}`, `offer_submit`, `offer_result {accepted}`); an item's variant reads `<colour>-custom` or `<colour>-upload`. The one exception is a shared link: a made-for-you print's address (`?make=`) carries what was typed into it (a name, a date, a city), so whoever gets the link can read it. When an analytics tag is added, keep `make` (and the search `q`) out of the page URLs it collects; analytics already sends only the referrer's origin.

## Analytics (`lib/analytics.ts`)

- **Attribution:** `landing` (utm_source / medium / campaign, ref, has_taste, has_list, landing_path, referrer) is recorded once per page load, before any page strips those tags from the address bar; the session's first touch is kept in sessionStorage and attached to `purchase`. `page_view` fires on every page, client-side navigations included.
- **Commerce, GA4 shape** (`trackEcommerce`: currency, value, items with item_id, item_name, item_category, item_variant = tee colour, size, price, quantity, discount): `view_item_list`, `select_item` and `view_item` (both with `item_list_id` when opened from a list), `add_to_cart`, `remove_from_cart`, `view_cart` (only when the bag itself is on screen, not when Buy now opens the form directly), `begin_checkout`, `add_shipping_info` (the form submitted valid), `purchase`. The pair counts as $90, its saving as the items' discount. Every add names its `source`: product, grid, minibag, cart_xsell, empty_bag, saved, confirm, discover_card, shared_list, me.
- **Product:** `swipe`, `undo`, `calibration_complete`, `taste_sheet_open`, `shop_view` (with the order actually shown), `shop_filter`, `search` (once per settled search — Enter, a chip or 1.5 s without typing: the words, up to 64 characters, how many results, whether it was corrected or relaxed, the facet kinds; its grid as the `shop_search` list), `select_size`, `save`, `share` and `share_taste` — shares only when they went through or the link was copied, never for a dismissed sheet.
- **Buying steps:** `buy_sheet_open` / `buy_sheet_dismiss`, `size_required {source}` (a buy button tapped without a size), `size_guide_open`, `pair_select {source}`, `minibag_checkout`, `sticky_checkout_click {from}`, `form_error {field, rule}` (the field and the rule broken, never the value), `share_banner_action {action}`.
- Nothing personal is sent (no name, email or address); `e2e/r2-stage3.spec.ts` walks a whole funnel and checks each event fires once.

## Brand

`components/MonoLogo.tsx` is the wordmark (capitals, wide tracking, no box); `app/icon.svg` is the favicon. One face for the interface, Geist (OFL, `public/fonts/geist*.woff2`, self-hosted with next/font/local), in regular and medium only, on the scale 12 / 14 / 16 / 20 / 28, sentence case everywhere but the wordmark. One corner radius for controls (2px), none for pictures, 16px for sheet tops; flat #0a0a0a, #f5f5f5 text, one muted tone (neutral-400), white/10 hairlines; 150 ms for UI and 250 ms for sheets on one easing, and `prefers-reduced-motion` turns movement into short fades. One button (`components/buttons.ts`: 48px, 14px medium). The decisions behind all this are in `docs/ux-overhaul.md`.
